# Game vision — Low Blow Legends

## One-line pitch

**A proper old-school 2D fighting game starring absolutely unqualified fictional champions who deploy smack talk, fourth-wall breaks and catastrophically disappointing finishing moves.**

## Identity

- Gameplay inspiration: genre-wide 1990s arcade fighting conventions. **Do not recreate Street Fighter II, Mortal Kombat, their trademarks, distinctive characters, audiovisual assets, stages or signature finishers.**
- Comedy inspiration: outrageous sports-style psyche-outs and self-aware absurdist satire; write new original jokes, not lifted film dialogue.
- Tone: roughly half tight arcade combat, half profane, absurd, sometimes deadpan comedy. Mature humour is optional, not the only way to be funny.
- Visual direction: readable original sprites/silhouettes, strong posing, industrial-arcade UI, restrained colours, and a distinctive identity. The teaser is not an art bible.

## Combat pillars

1. **Good fighter first.** Spacing, anti-airs, throws, recovery, blocking and round flow must be real systems.
2. **Psyche-outs are moves.** They consume a meter or create punishable commitment, with telegraphs, duration and counterplay. They may stun an avatar **inside the match**, not the actual user's controls.
3. **Finishers refuse the premise.** At KO, a character may undercut melodramatic finishers by speaking to the camera or doing an anticlimactic gag. Do not require or simulate graphic gore.
4. **Fourth-wall breaks keep clarity.** Dialogue and comedy must not conceal frame data, HUD state or results.
5. **Distinct original roster.** Every fighter needs different moves and motivations, rather than a real-person lookalike with a changed name.

## Candidate ORIGINAL archetypes (proposals, not approved final artwork)

- **Sir Flexington:** painfully earnest gym motivational guru whose inspirational speeches are more tiring than his punches.
- **Professor Fist:** over-explains Newtonian mechanics while getting punched in the face.
- **The Executive:** a corporate tyrant who attempts to outsource their attacks and litigate every hitbox.
- **Captain Conspiracy:** suspects the referee, the UI and possibly the spectator pigeons.
- **Patch Notes:** a self-modifying nerd who announces every move as a backwards-incompatible upgrade.
- **Auntie Mayhem:** an entirely fictional, deceptively polite community organiser with devastating counterthrows.

Keep them independently designed. A generic archetype name above is **not** approval to copy any real celebrity's appearance, outfit, voice, catchphrase or signature mannerisms.

## Proposed signature moments

- The announcer declares "FINISH THEM!" and a fighter answers, "Mate, they're already on the floor."
- "This finishing move needs seventeen button inputs. I'm just going to kick you in the nuts." (Comedic, non-graphic anticlimax.)
- "I don't think your hitbox has read the documentation."
- "The finishing animation is a premium feature. Neither of us bought it."
- At a losing streak: "WHOEVER IS CONTROLLING ME — HAVE YOU TRIED BLOCKING?"

These are **sample dialogue only**, not implemented gameplay or a mandatory final script.

## First vertical slice (v0.1.0)

A real local two-player/practice 1v1 arena: two truly original fighters, one original stage, standing/ducking, walking/jumping, blocking, light/heavy attacks, one simple combo, one psyche-out per fighter, timer, KO, rounds, restart and readable HUD. Practice AI is a stretch target; local keyboard gamepad support is desirable but must not obstruct accessible two-player controls.

Deliver working responsive combat before adding characters, network play, complicated finishers or advanced visual effects.

## Tone safety

We may mock vanity, bad logic, dubious competence and the video game itself. We do not target real private individuals, invite dogpiling, use recognizable imitations without permission, or treat close-call lookalikes as a loophole. Offensive jokes have to serve the comedy, not constitute harassment.
